# t-SNE over posts in the reader constellation (TIN-5680)

Authority: RM10 (TIN-3692 comment bb016df6), R-HOOK-CONVERGENCE-20261004 /
R-N13. TIN-5680 is a child of TIN-2413. Spec: #291
(`docs/agent-notes/2026-10-06-tsne-over-posts-spec.md`, 2ed0c119), option (c).
Base: main 0ed227cc. Production stays default-off behind
`src/lib/flags/constellation.ts`; nothing about the default page changes.

## What landed

- `src/lib/reader/projection/` holds the producer core and the browser view:
  - `text.ts`: term reduction;
  - `embed.ts`: corpus, similarity, edges, warm start, Procrustes, document;
  - `tsne.ts`: exact t-SNE;
  - `scene.ts`: the browser-safe join with routeable posts, plus arrow-key
    navigation.
- `scripts/generate-posts-projection.mts` writes the committed artifact
  `src/lib/data/posts-projection.v1.json`. The recipes are
  `just posts-projection` (warm), `just posts-projection-cold` and
  `just posts-projection-check`.
- `ReaderConstellation.svelte` gains two views, **Similarity map** (SVG, one
  roving tab stop, arrow keys to nearby posts, Escape, `?focus=<slug>`) and
  **Similar posts** (the list equivalent: every placed post with its nearest
  neighbours). The existing Constellation and Year tree views were unchanged
  and still the default. TIN-8628 removed both, see below.

## Decisions

1. **Committed artifact, not generated in CI.** Warm-start stability needs the
   previous coordinates as an input, and a committed file gives a reviewable
   diff when the map moves. The build therefore does no numerical work. CI
   fails when the method or configuration changes without a refit, or when the
   file holds a slug that is not `published: true`
   (`committed-projection.test.ts`). Since TIN-8628 CI also fails when the
   published posts no longer match the committed corpus: a new, edited or
   removed post needs `just posts-projection` and a commit of the result.
   `just check` runs `just posts-projection-check` first.
2. **TypeScript port, not the spear Python.** The method is spear
   `profile/refresh/embed.py` at 1b8b27863f90 (`tfidf-jaccard-exact-tsne-2`):
   the same weights, the same edge rule, the same warm-start rule and the same
   Procrustes step. It is ported so that neither the blog build nor CI needs the
   pinned numpy/scikit-learn shell. The coordinates are reproducible
   (byte-identical for the same inputs on one JS engine) but **not**
   bit-compatible with scikit-learn, because numpy's RandomState is replaced by
   seeded mulberry32 plus Box-Muller. The revision is recorded as
   `tfidf-jaccard-exact-tsne-2/ts-port-1`.
3. **Random init, not PCA.** The spec said `init="pca"`, but scikit-learn
   rejects PCA init with `metric="precomputed"`. spear uses seeded random init,
   and so does this port.
4. **Perplexity 15** (the spec's starting value), capped at (n-1)/3. Title,
   description and tag tokens carry weight 3. The body contributes its top 120
   terms after code, Svelte script/style, markup, link targets and URLs are
   removed. Dates, order, featured state and colour never enter the fit.
5. **No runtime fetch.** The JSON is a static import, about 14 KB minified
   before gzip. With the flag off, nothing renders and nothing is requested.
   Round three replaced the static import with one hashed data file that the
   section fetches lazily. See "Round three" below. With the flag off there is
   still no request.
6. **Plain SVG, not the #288 WebGL engine.** About 140 nodes do not need WebGL,
   and #288 is not merged. Round three draws on a 2D canvas over an inline SVG
   silhouette. It still needs no WebGL.

### The design in ten lines

1. A sky panel at full content width: 66 percent of the viewport height on a
   desktop and a tall panel on a phone. It has no border, no box and no fill of
   its own. Two soft tints and the glows of the constellations sit on the page
   itself, pale by day and dark at night.
2. One star per post. Colour is the category. Size is length in four steps.
   Glow is recency in three steps.
3. Ten named constellations, found and named at build time from what their
   posts share: Birding, Software, Personal, Nature observations, DevOps,
   Hardware, Tutorials, Music, Photography and Making. Every star in a figure
   carries the figure's name as its category or as a tag. Ten stars carry no
   group's name and stand outside every figure.
4. Each constellation is drawn as a sparse figure: a minimum spanning tree
   inside the group, without its overlong lines. 115 lines replace 243.
5. A pointer picks the nearest star. Hover shows a card. A click, a tap or
   keyboard focus pins it, with Open and up to three nearby posts as next steps.
   A second click or tap, Enter or Open opens the post.
6. Zoom needs intent: the buttons, a pinch, a double click, or Ctrl or Cmd with
   the wheel. A plain wheel scrolls the page. So does a one-finger swipe up or
   down, at rest and zoomed in. A name flies to its constellation. Reset
   returns.
7. The sky assembles once per session in 0.9 seconds: stars arrive left to
   right, glows and names fade in, figures draw. Reduced motion skips it.
8. One line of copy, a legend that filters, and a How it works popover that
   holds the how-to and the method note.
9. The first thing the section shows is the same stars as an inline SVG
   silhouette in a panel that already has its final size. The interactive layer
   loads over it later. Under the flag the section itself arrives after the
   page has painted. See "Round four", findings 1 and 2.
10. All layout is done by the generator and committed, the place and the zoom
    of every post title included. The browser only applies transforms. The
    session remembers the view, the selection and the filter.

### Decisions

1. **Groups by Ward clustering on the map itself.** The groups are what the
   reader sees, so they are cut from the drawn positions, about twelve posts
   each. A name is the label most of a group shares and few posts outside it
   carry, and at least half of the group must carry it. Two touching groups
   that earn the same name merge. The five catch-all tags of the old WordPress
   site (Featured, Reviews, Ideas, How-To and DIY) never name a group. A named
   group then keeps only the stars that carry its name, so no figure claims a
   post its name is not true of.
2. **The map is turned and each axis fills the panel.** The long axis of the
   fit goes across a desktop and down a phone. The phone frame is the desktop
   frame transposed. This is honest only because axes and distances were never
   measurements, and the help says so. No grid is drawn, for the same reason.
3. **Eight hues turned from the theme's primary.** Star colours are
   `oklch(from var(--color-primary-500) L C calc(h + n * 45))`. Neighbouring
   hues alternate between two lightness steps, so two categories side by side
   on the wheel still tell apart. Paper is the theme's page background. Ink,
   card, accent and shadow are theme surface and primary tokens or mixes of
   them. Round four removed the literal white, black and shadow colours that
   round three still had, in the styles and in the canvas. A browser without
   relative colour syntax draws every star in the theme's primary. The category
   is also in the legend, in the card and in each star's accessible name, so
   colour is never the only signal.
4. **Size is word count, glow is recency.** Both are true of the post and cost
   one small integer each. A photo on focus was left out: 92 of the 140 posts
   share a stock header photo, so it would say nothing about the post.
5. **Select, then open, for every pointer.** RM26 ruled this for touch. Round
   three extends it to the mouse, where a click used to open at once, so that
   the card with Open and the nearby posts can be reached. Ctrl or Cmd click
   still opens a new tab.
6. **Zoom needs Ctrl or Cmd, or two fingers.** The map never traps page scroll.
   The map's `touch-action` is `pan-y` at every zoom, so a one-finger swipe up
   or down always scrolls the page. Zoomed in, a mouse drag pans, a one-finger
   drag that starts sideways pans, and two fingers pan and zoom. Round three
   set `touch-action: none` once zoomed, which did trap a phone reader.
7. **Canvas over an SVG silhouette.** The silhouette is two inline SVGs, one
   per frame, with each star a near zero-length round-capped stroke that does
   not scale. It needs no script. The canvas maps stars onto the same box, and
   its first frame draws them at the silhouette's opacity, so nothing jumps.
   Everything soft (the glow of each constellation and the halo of each star)
   is drawn once into its own canvas and then only moved by a CSS transform.
   A frame fills small shapes and blends nothing large.
8. **One tab stop for each part.** The Map and List tabs come first in the
   section, so the list is one tab stop and one arrow key away. The stars share
   one roving tab stop, the constellation names another. Arrow keys move
   between nearby stars, Home and End go to the first and last, Enter opens,
   Escape clears, plus and minus zoom.
9. **Type the home page already loads.** The section uses Inter at 600 and 700
   and the body face. An earlier pass used Inter 400, which made the page fetch
   one more font file. That was removed.
10. **No cache header was added.** The data file and the interactive chunk are
    under `/_app/immutable/` with a content hash in their names, so they are
    safe to cache for good. The site has no `_headers` file today. Adding one
    changes caching for the default page, which needs an operator check-in
    under the repository operator notes.
11. **Skip to Latest was removed.** The page navigation above the section
    already links to Latest, and the map now takes few tab stops.

### The design in eight lines

1. The sky is the first thing in the page's main column and runs the full width
   of the viewport: 86 percent of its height on a desktop, and the visible
   height below the site header on a phone. It has no fill, border or edge of
   its own. One band fades out at its top and its foot, so the page background
   and the sky join with no seam. The profile column starts below it.
2. Nothing is printed above the stars. A legend of a dot and one word for each
   category lies along the foot and is the filter. Five icon controls sit in
   one corner: list, zoom in, zoom out, reset and help. Each has a tooltip and
   an accessible name.

### Decisions

1. **A constellation is a category that sits together.** Round three named
   groups from tags, so the legend said Ecology where the sky said Birding and
   Nature observations. Now two stars of one category within six median
   nearest-star gaps are joined, and each joined set of three or more is a
   constellation. The largest of a category carries the category's label. So
   every star in a figure has the figure's name as its own category, and a
   name on the sky is a word in the legend, in the colour of its stars. The
   140 posts give eight named constellations (Ecology 34, Personal 20,
   Software 19, Hardware 14, Tutorials 14, DevOps 14, Music 7, Photography 6),
   two small Hardware figures with no name and six stars outside every figure.
   The Ward grouping, the tag labels and the merge rule are deleted.
2. **The sky goes before the page's column.** `+page.svelte` renders the
   section ahead of the "Writing and notes." header, under the site banner, so
   the page opens onto it. The section measures the page once and sets its own
   width and offset, so it is exact with or without a scrollbar. The default
   page has the same markup as before.
3. **The profile column starts below the sky.** On a desktop the layout puts a
   sticky profile panel beside the main column. A full-bleed sky under it would
   have a box lying over its stars. The section sets the column's top padding
   to its own height and clears it when the flag goes off.
4. **Focus shows a tooltip and does not pin.** Round three pinned a star on
   focus. Now arrow keys show the tooltip, Enter opens, and Space pins the
   card, as a click does. A pin does not follow the keyboard to another star.
5. **The card looks for free space.** `placeCard` tries the four spots beside,
   above and below the star, then a grid of spots 20 pixels apart out to 220
   pixels, and takes the cheapest. Covering the star or one of the three
   nearest posts is ruled out, the lines to them cost most, other stars and
   names cost a little, and every 30 pixels of distance costs 1. A card that
   had to move away is tied to its star by a thin line. A card that is placed
   stays unless another spot is clearly better.
6. **A summary that is only a web address is not shown.** `summaryOf` cuts
   addresses out of a summary and keeps what is left only when at least three
   words remain. A summary with no address is left as it is.
7. **Arrival without a shift, with and without motion.** The flag is still read
   after the first paint. With reduced motion the content below fades out
   where it was, moves while it cannot be seen, and fades in with the sky.
8. **Controls that cannot act stay focusable.** They carry `aria-disabled`, so
   the group keeps one tab stop whatever the map's state.
9. **Pulse under the flag.** The page's Pulse section takes the broker's
   reviewed snapshot once it has loaded. Without the flag it is unchanged.
10. **The map fades out under the legend and the controls.** Neither has a box
    behind it. The stars, the glows and the names share one mask, which ends
    above the strip at the foot and, on a phone, below the controls at the
    top. At rest no star is in either strip. Zoomed in, a star that moves
    there fades out, so no word or icon is read through a star.
11. **The tooltip tries four spots.** `placeTip` offers the side the star's
    title would take, the other side, above and below, and leaves out a spot
    that does not fit. The component counts the stars, the names and the
    titles of other stars each spot would hide and takes the least. The
    painter keeps the box of every title it drew for this. At the right edge
    of the map the flipped tooltip lay over six stars in the second look. It
    now takes the spot that hides the fewest.
12. **The legend says its counts on hover and focus.** The counts left the
    page. Each entry keeps its count in its accessible name and in a tooltip.
13. **A name and the legend never disagree on a count.** The legend counts
    every post of a category. A constellation can hold fewer: Ecology 34 of
    35, Personal 20 of 21 and Hardware 14 of 24. In the third look the legend
    said "21 posts" for Personal and the name on the sky said "20 posts". A
    name now says "20 of 21 posts" on hover, on focus and in its accessible
    name. A constellation that holds every post of its category says only the
    count.
14. **The sky does not follow the header's last pixel.** On a phone the sky is
    the visible height less the site header. The header gains a one pixel
    edge once the page scrolls past the banner. The sky keeps the height it
    took, so nothing moves under a reader who is scrolling. It follows a
    header that changes height for another reason, such as a font that loads
    late.
15. **Dynamic viewport units, as asked.** The heights use `dvh`, so a phone's
    browser bars never cut the sky. On a phone whose bars slide away during a
    scroll the sky grows with the visible height. That needs a look on a real
    phone, and `svh` is the fallback if it reads as a jump.

16. **A narrow phone reserves three legend rows.** At 320 pixels the eight
    words wrap to three rows, and the e2e suite found the third row lying
    over three stars. Up to 350 pixels the stars, the mask and the list keep
    clear of three rows.

### Decisions

1. **One block in `app.html`, for her to check.** The flag is read in the
   browser, so only a script that runs before the first paint can keep the
   room. The block sits inside the script element that already sets the theme
   there. It adds no request and no script element. It runs on every page, and
   it sets `data-sky` on the document only when the path is `/` and the flag is
   on. A unit test runs the block against `resolveConstellationFlag` for 24
   cases and holds the two to the same answer. The resolver still decides
   after mount, and the page clears the mark when it says no. Without the
   block the choice is between a layout shift and an entrance.
2. **The page pads, the section lies in the padding.** `+page.svelte` gives
   `--sky-h` to the document and pads `#main-content` and the profile column by
   it. The section is placed at the top of the main column, out of the flow,
   and takes its height from the same property. One rule sets both, so they
   cannot disagree. The script that set the profile column's padding is gone.
3. **The header's height is in the styles.** Below 768 pixels the site header
   is 84 pixels tall at every width measured, so the phone height is the
   visible height less 5.25rem with no script. From 768 pixels up the header's
   height varies with its wrap, between 80 and 94 pixels, and the sky takes 86
   percent of the height as a desktop does. Round five used the phone rule up
   to 880 pixels and measured the header in script. That measure came after
   the first paint and moved the room by 4 to 10 pixels on a tablet.
4. **The shell is a chunk of its own under the flag.** The silhouette then
   comes one request after the page's own code. At launch the section is in
   the page as built, the import goes back to a plain one and the silhouette
   is in the first paint.
5. **The upper right stays empty.** The fit puts Ecology apart from the rest,
   and the gap between them is real. A spread of each axis by rank was tried on
   the data and moved fewer than five stars into the empty part. Moving stars
   to fill a frame would say something the fit does not.
6. **The banner fades at its foot only under the flag.** The fade is 4 percent
   of the viewport's width, at most 5rem, so on a phone it stays clear of the
   banner's last line. Without the flag the banner is as it was.
7. **What the contrast case measures.** The star's colour against the page,
   its moving background, the band and the two tints. A star's own halo and
   its constellation's glow are left out: they are tints of the star's own
   colour, and the pixel under a new star is nearly the star itself. At the
   middle of a glow the sky is at most 13 percent nearer the star's colour,
   which by arithmetic takes the lowest figure from 3.70 to about 3.4.
8. **A summary of the motion that is left.** With motion allowed: the section
   fades in once, the stars assemble once a session, and the map flies to a
   name. With reduced motion: none of these.
9. **With reduced motion nothing in the section transitions.** The site's
   reduced-motion rule sets every transition to a hundredth of a millisecond.
   It does not turn them off, so each style change as the section loaded was
   still an animation in the browser's list, 280 of them in one frame for the
   star links. The section now sets `transition-property: none` on itself and
   everything in it under reduced motion. The arrival case reads the list for
   30 frames from the frame the section is put in and expects it empty.
10. **Two e2e cases measured too early, and both are fixed in the case.** The
    contrast case read the page while the silhouette still lay under the
    canvas, so it measured a star against its own silhouette. It now waits for
    the silhouette to go. The phone case that picks a star after two zooms
    took the links as still after 250 milliseconds. A zoom flies for 220 and
    the links follow 150 after it ends, so it now waits 450.
